1Tricks — review · 3 confirmed, 5 challenged, 1 mixed
The draft is sound where it matters most: the three animation families are the right three, the ledger is the right idea, the picker copy is nearly all keepable, and the zero-stakes contract is stated in the interface rather than only in the notes. The challenges below are concentrated in one place — Tricks borrows four colours that already mean something else, and each borrowing costs its meaning on a screen that needs it more. Corrected artboards are in section 2, live. Part 1's build report is answered in section 3.
1aVerdicts
1 · New vocabulary2 CHALLENGED · 1 CONFIRMEDSEVERITY: HIGH
Dirt for the counting block — challenged, replaced. Dirt #7a5233 and Wood #a9713c are the same brown at arm's length, and Nines.dc.html renders the block as a bevelled square at 22px — the tier badge's shape at nearly the tier badge's size. A hundred and twenty brown bevelled squares says Wood tier to a child taught to read materials as mastery, and says it on the one screen where mastery is explicitly not measured. RULED: the counting block is paper — card ground #fffdf6, 3px ink border, the keypad's bevel at 22px. The framing that settles it: Tricks is the notebook, not the inventory. Its blocks are drawn, not mined. The texture trim at the top of each page keeps the world without spending a material on it.
The ten-frame — confirmed. Ink border plus a Jersey tag is the inventory-slot idea applied to a group instead of a thing, and it costs no colour. Two conditions: the border stays ink, never an accent, so grouping reads as structure; and the tag sits at 13px, the Jersey floor, used nowhere else on the page.
Slate for the taken-away row — challenged, replaced. This is the most consequential item in the review. The draft uses #dfe6ee on #8ba4bb — --wrong-pale on --wrong-edge, the send-back palette exactly. Slate is load-bearing precisely because it means one thing everywhere: not yet, we'll come back to it. In ×9 the removed row is the method working. Teaching that removal and error look alike costs nothing here and costs a great deal on the card screens, where slate is doing the hardest job in the app. RULED: removal is depicted as absence, not as a colour. Fill drops out, border goes to #cfc8b2, opacity to .35, border width unchanged so nothing reflows. No palette entry spent, slate untouched.
2 · Green disciplineCHALLENGEDSEVERITY: HIGH
Three uses in the draft, and they are not one question. The result wash is out. s3 resolves to color:#3d7a22; background:#eaf5df — the correct-answer object, the thing that appears when she answered and was right. Here the app is demonstrating and she has done nothing. RULED: the result reuses the neutral problem slab — card ground, 3px ink, stone trim, big Jersey. That object already means here is the fact, and it costs no new vocabulary.
The answer pop is out too, and it is the subtler of the two. ansStyle springs scale(.4) → 1 on cubic-bezier(.34,1.56,.64,1) — that is --ease-pop, the correct-answer burst's own curve. Reward motion on an unearned event is the same error as reward colour. The result fades and rises 6px on --ease-out instead.
The selected chip keeps green, as an outline. Choosing an n is her acting, so green is right — but the draft fills it #eaf5df, which is the correct-answer fill. RULED: 3px #57a636 outline on card ground, borrowed verbatim from deck select's chosen-mode treatment. Outline means she chose; fill means she was right. Two states, two treatments, no overlap anywhere in the app.
3 · MotionCHALLENGEDSEVERITY: MEDIUM
The ambient loop is out; the instinct behind it survives. A loop erases its own conclusion on a schedule she does not control: she reads = 63 and the array wipes out from under her. The frame a loop guarantees she will lose is the only frame that matters. Worse in the draft as written — Nines phase 0 sets every row to opacity:0 for 700ms, so the stage goes blank once per cycle; a child glancing at the page at the wrong moment sees an empty card.
RULED: plays once on arrival, then holds the finished state, with Again ›. No tap needed to see it the first time, so wandering in mid-frustration still costs nothing — that was the right instinct and it does not need a loop. A held result also makes the page a genuine reference: she can glance at ×9 and read the answer with the structure still under it, without waiting for a phase to come round. Tapping a new chip replays.
Timings — the transitions are right and the pacing is not. 240–300ms per transition stands, transform and opacity only. But Nines waits [700, 2400, 2000, 2800] spends 7.9s per cycle mostly on holds around an animation that is over in a moment, while the build itself staggers ten rows at 70ms — 630ms for the whole array, too fast to count along with. RULED: build 900ms staggered · hold 600 · transform 300 · hold 900 · result 300 · hold indefinitely. About 3s to a held result, and the row stagger slow enough to follow. Under prefers-reduced-motion the page renders the held result immediately with the ledger fully lit; Again › stays and does nothing visible, which is correct rather than broken. The draft's blanket transition-duration:0 override achieves this by accident — make it deliberate.
4 · The ledgerCONFIRMEDSEVERITY: LOW
Jersey 25 for the whole ledger, word phrases included. take away 7 is not a sentence — it is an operation that happens to be spelled, stacked between two lines that are unambiguously equations. Splitting the stack across two fonts would be worse than either font alone.
The test this sets for the remaining eight pages: if it would appear on a blackboard as part of the working, it is Jersey; prose about the trick stays Nunito; the two never share a block. The draft already honours this — the explainer under each header is Nunito and the ledger is Jersey. One change: ledger lines go 20px → 22px and unlit lines go from #d5d2c9 to ink at opacity .26. Pale grey on parchment is close to invisible in daylight, and the ledger is the second-most-looked-at object on the page.
5 · Layout worst casesCHALLENGEDSEVERITY: HIGH
Adaptive block size is out. Fours drops 22px → 18px above n=10. If a block is one size on one card and another size on the next, the block stops being a unit and the array stops being comparable — which is the only thing an array is for. She must see 4 × 11 as bigger than 4 × 6, not as the same size built from smaller bricks.
RULED: 22px blocks, 3px gaps, constant across every trick and every n. Twelve columns is 12×22 + 11×3 = 297px against 354px available at an 18px gutter — 57px spare, so no trick ever needs to shrink. Reflowing an arrangement is not the same as resizing a unit and stays allowed: Fives at n=12 is six ten-frames, which is 379px tall in one column and does not fit, so above three frames it goes to two columns at 278px wide and 187px tall. The block never changes; the arrangement may.
Fours' side tags are the actual defect and they move. The width:30px tags beside each group are what forced the shrink in the first place. Side tags are out — running totals belong in the ledger, which already carries them line by line. Any caption a trick needs goes under the array, full width, so the array always owns the whole column.
Chips — confirmed, and tighter than it looks. Six columns at a 16px gutter with 4px gaps gives 56.3px per chip. That clears --tap-comfy by a third of a pixel, which is correct but has no margin at all; the corrected artboards keep the 4px gaps and drop the gutter to 12px, for 57.7px. The gutter is what buys the margin, not the gap — widening the gap spends the same pixels the chips needed. Rows of six then five, left-aligned. It is the only element allowed outside the 18px gutter, and the reason is the touch minimum, which outranks the gutter.
6 · Entry and placementMIXED · RULEDSEVERITY: HIGH
Copy — confirmed, with one rule added. No timer, no score — just shortcuts states the contract in eight words and never says help, which would make arriving an admission. Keep it verbatim, but move it under the header, not the grid: it is the page's promise and it should be read before the tiles, not after. Tile copy names the action — take a row away, fives make tens, slide in a zero — and the standing rule is that no tile may ever say hard, tricky, tough, or the difficult one. The tables she avoids are exactly the tiles she must be able to tap without flinching. Two small corrections: sentence case rather than the draft's shouted all-caps, which reads as instruction rather than invitation; and The Flip leaves the grid — it is not a table and should not sit in the table grid.
Placement — RULED: deck select, and Round cleared. Not Home, and not the wrong-answer state.
Deck select is the primary door: a fourth row under the three decks, book icon, no DUE chip and no tier bar, because it is a place rather than a deck. She is already choosing how to work when she is there.
Home is refused. Home has one job. A Tricks row competes with the green button and dilutes YOUR MATERIALS, whose whole argument is that mastery is the reason to practise — that was the case for deleting the tab bar six weeks of decisions ago, and a second door on Home reopens it.
The wrong-answer state is refused, and this is the one to argue about. The candidate is genuinely tempting — she is exactly then in need of a trick. But the wrong state is slate, silent and still, and that is the load-bearing rule of the entire app: nothing appears on a failure-adjacent state. A pickaxe affordance on a missed card turns a miss into an event, and hands her an exit at the precise moment four sections of design have spent making the miss unremarkable. The compensation is better than the thing refused: Round cleared already knows what she missed, so it offers the trick for the table she missed most — ×9 came up twice. Want the trick? — as a quiet stone link. Same need, roughly sixty seconds later, at the breather, aimed at the right table instead of a generic door.
The picker's bottom edge — RULED: nothing. The draft's tab bar goes, per 5a. The picker is a push from deck select and returns with ‹; trick pages push from the picker and return to it. No bar, no chip, no parent key — the parent door lives on Home alone. The bottom edge is the closing line of the contract instead: Come here any time. Nothing here is counted.
7 · Extension to ×10 and The FlipCHALLENGEDSEVERITY: MEDIUM
The canvas notes say those two keep this exact page anatomy. Half right, and the half that is wrong needs building in now rather than discovered at ×10.
The frame holds. Header, explainer, stage, ledger, result slab, chip strip and Again › are trick-agnostic and carry all eleven pages plus The Flip.
The array does not. ×10 has no array transform — sliding a zero onto 7 to make 70 is a digit operation, and seventy blocks would bury the one thing it teaches. RULED: the stage is a slot, not a component. The page accepts any stage that plays in phases and reports which ledger line is lit. Two stages are needed at the outset: array, which carries ten of the twelve, and digits, which carries ×10 alone.
Superseded 30 Aug — ×11 moves to the array stage. I had ×11 on digits alongside ×10, and the ×11 tile named say the digit twice. Both were wrong: the digit pattern only holds for 2–9, and the tile would have been false on 11 × 10, 11 × 11 and 11 × 12 — the hardest corner of the deck. ×11 is now ten rows and one more, the mirror of ×9, with the digit pattern named as a payoff under the result rather than claimed as the trick. Full ruling in ELEVENS_RULING.md.
The Flip needs one thing beyond a stage. Rotating an array in place is a transform and obeys the rules, but its ledger is not three sequential lines — it is two equal statements, 7 × 8 = 56 and 8 × 7 = 56, and they must land together. RULED: a two-line simultaneous ledger variant, both lines lighting on one frame. A sequential reveal would imply the second derives from the first, which is exactly the misconception the trick exists to remove.
Restated because a new area is where doctrine leaks: no red anywhere · nothing punitive · celebration colour and celebration motion never appear where she has not acted · no border-radius · Jersey 25 never below 13px · transform and opacity only · the three greens do not drift, #57a636 correct, #7fe237 FAST, #12c46b emerald. Tricks adds no sound at all — the app has four cues, every one of them for something she did, and watching is not one of them.

3Part 1 — the 5a build report
3aSix deviations · 5 confirmed, 1 mixed
On §9.1 — the factors argument was wrong and it is worth saying so plainly. I argued the deletion would give the factors card room. The bar never mounted on card screens, so it gains nothing. The other reasons stand on their own and 5a survives, but that one should come out of the record rather than sit in it as a claim that happens to be false.
3 · Coins frame omitted from 2nCONFIRMED
Right call, and my artboard was wrong. The spec says the two apps are independent; showing a coin balance this app cannot read would be a design fiction. The caption carries what the two-frame layout was actually for — that emeralds are a separate collection — in fewer pixels. No frameless alternative needed.
4 · Empty-state footer copyCONFIRMED
Accept the substitution; do not grow the data model. A promotions-today count would be schema, migration and backup surface bought for one footer line, and the closest-promotion sentence is the better line anyway — it points forward at something reachable rather than back at something finished. Standing rule: no artboard sentence is worth a schema change. If copy needs data the model lacks, the copy changes.
5 · Armed chevron instead of a dialogCONFIRMED
Better than what I specified. A dialog is a modal interruption on a screen whose entire design is about not making events out of things; an armed chevron asks the same question without seizing the screen, and self-disarming on a keypad tap means carrying on with the round is itself the answer. Releasing banked emeralds through the normal award screen is the right fix and it is not a small one — silently discarding earned emeralds would have been the app's first punitive act.
6 · Quantizations to the token scaleCONFIRMED
All four. 13/14 → 12/16 and 10.5 → 12 are the scale doing its job; a canvas value that exists only because I typed it should lose to a token. WEARING at 13px is the Jersey floor and the floor wins. Where a canvas number and a token disagree by a step, the token is right by default — flag it only when the artboard number was load-bearing, which none of these were.
7 · The Overworld strip bugCONFIRMED
Good find, and the right fix — a url() in a custom property resolving against the using stylesheet is exactly the kind of thing a canvas cannot surface, because the canvas inlines everything. Reference-only strip tokens with the comment saying so is correct. It also sharpens the rule from last round: theme tokens carry colour; theme images are set per theme in the stylesheet.
8 · Built Home is simpler than 2aMIXED · RULED
Mostly my problem, not yours. Taking them one at a time so no new work order is needed for three of the four:
Greeting and date row — withdrawn. Canvas atmosphere. The clock is already there and she knows her own name.
Mastered progress bar — withdrawn. YOUR MATERIALS carries mastery now, and two mastery readouts on one screen is one too many.
"Pick a deck instead" — withdrawn. PRACTICE › pushing deck select is cleaner than a green button plus an escape link under it, and it is one fewer decision on the screen that should ask for none.
The DUE chip and the minutes estimate — worth a small order, and only these two. 18 DUE and About 4 minutes do work the rest of that list does not: they answer how long is this going to take before she commits, which is the single question that decides whether a distractible ten-year-old taps the button on a bad day. A bounded, visible, small number is the difference between a chore and an errand. Everything else on 2a can stay canvas-only.
